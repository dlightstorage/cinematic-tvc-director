Two halves, deliberately separated.

**Claim tagging.** Statements are tagged one of two ways:

|---|---|

**One structural warning before you start.** A production bible looks like a book. It is not a book. The moment you write a bible as though it has a reader who starts at page one, you have built something that fails the person who opens it at 6:40am on a shoot day looking for one number. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A1. WHAT A PRODUCTION BIBLE IS, AND WHO ACTUALLY READS IT

A production bible, sometimes called a crew bible, a world bible, a show bible or a series bible depending on the format, is the single consolidated reference document that holds every settled decision about a production that more than one department needs to know. **[PRINCIPLE]**

It is not the script. The script says what happens. It is not the shot list. The shot list says how it is covered. It is not the schedule. The schedule says when. The bible says **what the world is, what the rules of that world are, and who is responsible for each part of it.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The defining reading behavior

The single most important thing to understand about a bible is the way it is consumed.

A treatment has one reader who reads it once, in order, to be persuaded. A script has readers who read it in order to follow a story. A bible has **many readers, each of whom reads only one part, at an unpredictable time, in an unpredictable order, usually while something else is going wrong.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Reader | What they open the bible to find | When they open it |
|---|---|---|
| **Production designer / art director** | What each space physically looks like, what the palette is, what has to be built versus dressed versus found | Prep, and again at every set dress |
| **Set decorator / buyer** | Concrete purchasable descriptions of dressing per space | While standing in a market or a prop house with a budget |
| **Gaffer / DP** | The lighting recipe per space, practical sources, color temperature intent | The night before, and on the floor when a setup changes |
| **Costume supervisor** | What each person wears, how many changes, continuity between scenes | Fitting days, and every morning of the shoot |
| **Makeup and hair** | Per-person treatment, aging, condition, continuity notes | Call time, in a mirror room, with minutes available |
| **1st AD** | Which people and which spaces are involved in which units of work | While building and rebuilding the schedule |
| **Line producer** | What each decision costs and which flagged risks carry money | Budget rounds, and every change request |
| **Editor and post** | The intended screen time per space and per person, what was promised | Assembly, when something appears to be missing |
| **Client or brand custodian** | Whether the agreed brand rules survived into execution | Review milestones, usually late and unannounced |
| **Casting** | Role definitions, demographic and physical requirements, what each role has to do | Casting sessions and callbacks |

None of these people are reading for the story. Every one of them is running a lookup. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The operational test of a good bible

**Can a department head open the document cold, find their own section without asking anyone, and act on it without needing any other section?** **[PRINCIPLE]** If they have to read three other sections to make one decision, the bible has failed. If they have to ask the director a clarifying question that the bible should have answered, the bible has failed.

This test is unforgiving and it is the correct test. It rules out most elegant prose. It rules out most cleverness. It rules out almost every form of cross-reference. It is the reason bibles look the way they look.

### What a bible is actually for, in order of importance

1. **To stop the same question being asked twenty times.** A decision made once and written once is cheaper than a decision re-litigated in every department meeting. **[PRINCIPLE]**
2. **To make consistency survivable across time and people.** A production that runs weeks or months, with crew rotating in and out, cannot hold consistency in anyone's head. **[PRINCIPLE]**
3. **To convert taste into instructions.** "Warm and lived-in" is taste. "Tungsten practicals at 2700K, no overhead fluorescents, walls in aged plaster with visible repair patches" is an instruction. The bible is the machine that performs that conversion. **[PRINCIPLE]**
4. **To create a defensible record.** When a client says the palette changed, the bible is the artifact that says whether it did. **[PRINCIPLE]**
5. **To enable delegation.** A bible is what allows a department to be trusted to work unsupervised. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A2. DOCUMENTED EXAMPLES, AND WHAT THEY CONTAINED

### The feature-film world bible

The best documented modern example is the bible built by production designer **Hannah Beachler** for the fictional nation in the 2018 Marvel film *Black Panther*. Beachler assembled an extensive world-building document, widely reported as running to hundreds of pages, covering the invented nation's geography, history, tribal groups, architecture, technology and cultural rules, and it functioned as the shared reference for the design departments. Beachler went on to win the Academy Award for Best Production Design for that film, becoming the first Black artist to win in that category. **[SOURCED]**

Two things about that example matter more than its scale.

First, **it was built to be consulted, not admired.** Its function was to let dozens of artists, model makers, costume designers and VFX vendors make thousands of independent small decisions that would still cohere. That is the load a bible carries. **[PRINCIPLE]**

Second, **it defined rules, not just images.** A mood board says what something looks like. A bible says what is and is not permitted in that world, so that a decision nobody anticipated can still be made correctly by someone working alone. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The television series bible

The series bible, sometimes called a show bible, is a long-standing and well documented artifact in episodic television, used both as a pitch document for buyers and as a continuity reference for writers' rooms and incoming directors. Historic examples include the writer and director guidelines produced for network series in the 1960s onward, which set out format, tone, recurring character rules and what stories the show would and would not tell. **[SOURCED]**

A series bible typically contains: the premise and format, the world and its rules, the series-length arc, character biographies with what each character wants and what each will never do, recurring locations, tone and voice rules, and episode structure conventions. **[SOURCED]**

The series bible has an additional consumer that the feature bible does not: **the freelance writer or guest director who arrives mid-run and must produce work consistent with hundreds of prior decisions they were not present for.** That reader is the purest form of the out-of-order specialist reader, and the series bible's structure is largely shaped by them. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The animation and game bible

In animation and games the equivalent artifact is usually split into a **style guide** (how things are drawn, colored, proportioned and animated) and a **world or lore bible** (what exists, what the rules are, what the history is). Style guides are a documented industry norm for licensed properties, where third-party manufacturers and partners must reproduce a look without access to the original artists. **[SOURCED]** That is the extreme case of the out-of-order reader: a licensee in another country who will never speak to anyone on the creative team and has only the document. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The commercial and branded-content bible

In advertising the equivalent is the combination of the brand guidelines, the campaign toolkit and the production bible built for a specific shoot. It is smaller, shorter-lived and denser than a feature bible, and it is discussed in A12. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### What all of them share

Every one of these artifacts exists because **a group of people who cannot all be in the same conversation must nevertheless make consistent decisions.** The document is a substitute for a conversation that cannot scale. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A3. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE FOR OUT-OF-ORDER READERS

This is the core of the craft, and it is where most bibles fail.

### The five properties of a random-access document

A document read out of order by specialists under pressure needs five properties that a linear document does not. **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Property | What it means | What breaks without it |
|---|---|---|
| **Addressability** | Every fact lives at a findable location with a stable name or number | Readers scan, miss things, and ask the director instead |
| **Locality of meaning** | A section is comprehensible without the sections before it | The reader has to read four sections to act on one |
| **Shape consistency** | Two comparable sections have the same internal structure | The reader must learn a new layout for every department |
| **Self-containment** | No entry depends on another entry being read first | An out-of-order reader gets a pointer instead of an answer |
| **Single sourcing per fact** | Each fact has exactly one authoritative home, and copies are managed deliberately | Two places disagree and nobody knows which is current |

Notice that **self-containment and single sourcing pull against each other.** That tension is the central design problem of the bible, and it is resolved in A7. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Why the front-to-back instinct is so persistent and so wrong

Writers are trained on linear forms. The instinct is to build an argument: establish context, then develop, then conclude. In a bible this produces sections that only make sense if you read the preceding ones, which is exactly the reading pattern that will not happen. **[PRINCIPLE]**

The correct mental model is closer to a **parts catalogue or a field manual** than to a book. A parts catalogue does not build an argument. It answers "what is this part, what does it fit, what does it cost, what is its number" for every part, in the same shape every time, and it is never read in order. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The pressure condition

The bible is often consulted at the worst possible moment: on a shoot day, on a phone screen, with a crew waiting. This has three concrete consequences. **[PRINCIPLE]**

1. **Scanning beats reading.** Headings, tables and bolded keys are load bearing, not decoration.
2. **Depth of navigation is a cost.** Every hop the reader must make to reach an answer is a place where they give up and guess.
3. **Ambiguity resolves against you.** A reader under time pressure who finds a vague entry does not stop and ask. They act on their own interpretation, and you find out on the edit.

### Ordering principle

Order the bible by **who needs it**, not by narrative sequence. A conventional and reliable order: **[PRINCIPLE]**

1. Purpose, scope, version and owner (one page, always first)
2. The rules of the world (the short list of non-negotiables that govern everything else)
3. The master reference tables (people, spaces, and any third axis that earns one)
4. Per-department entries, in a fixed order that does not change between versions
5. Schedules, logistics and contacts
6. Appendices: references, sources, glossary, change log

Master tables sit **before** department entries because they are the most frequently consulted part of the document and because department entries frequently point into them. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A4. THE PER-DEPARTMENT ENTRY, AND WHY A TEMPLATE BEATS BESPOKE PROSE

A bible contains one entry per department: art, wardrobe, makeup and hair, camera, lighting, sound, stunts, VFX, transport, catering, and so on.

The temptation is to write each one in whatever shape suits that department, because departments genuinely differ. Wardrobe thinks in people and changes. Lighting thinks in spaces and times of day. Sound thinks in scenes and sources. Bespoke prose feels respectful of those differences.

**It is the wrong call, and the reason is the comparing reader.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The comparing reader argument

The reader who suffers most from bespoke entries is not the department head reading their own section. It is the person reading **two or more sections at once**: the producer costing three departments, the 1st AD checking which departments are involved in a given unit of work, the director checking that four departments agree about the same moment, the new crew member trying to understand where they fit.

For that reader, **inconsistent structure is a tax paid on every comparison.** If wardrobe puts its risk flags at the end and art puts them in the middle of a paragraph, the reader has to learn two documents to do one job, and will miss one of the flags. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The second argument: absence becomes visible

A fixed template makes **missing information detectable**. If every department entry has a "budget owner" field and one of them is blank, the blank is visible. If the entries are free prose, the absence of a budget owner is invisible, because nothing on the page indicates that a budget owner was ever expected. **[PRINCIPLE]**

This is the single strongest practical argument for templates. **A template turns an unknown unknown into a visible blank.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The third argument: the template is a checklist for the writer

The person building the bible is also under pressure and also working out of order. The template tells them what they still owe. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The correct amount of flexibility

Templates fail when they are enforced so rigidly that a department's real content will not fit. The working compromise: **fixed field names and fixed field order for every department, with the content inside a field free to be as long or short as it needs to be, plus one final open field for anything that genuinely does not fit.** **[PRINCIPLE]** The open field is what prevents people from smuggling important content into the wrong field to satisfy the form.

## A5. WHEN A TABLE BEATS PROSE

This is the distinction that most bible builders get wrong, and it is worth stating precisely.

> **A consistent per-section template helps when the sections are about different things.**
> **A table helps when the sections are about the same things.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The test

Ask: **do several parts of this document describe the same fixed set of entities from different angles?**

If yes, that is a table. The entities are the rows, the angles are the columns.

If no, if each section is about a genuinely different subject, then a templated section is correct and a table would force unlike things into a shape that distorts them.

### Worked distinction

Departments are different things. Art is not a variant of sound. They share a shape (each has a lead, a budget, deliverables, risks) but their content is not commensurable, and you would not want a row per department with a column called "palette" that is empty for eleven of them. **Departments get templated sections.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

People are the same kind of thing as each other. Every person in the production has a role, a screen presence, a wardrobe treatment, a makeup treatment, and a set of spaces they appear in. Those are the same five questions asked of every one of them. **People get a table.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

Spaces are the same kind of thing as each other. Every space has a physical description, a set of shots, a duration on screen, a lighting approach, a palette, a prep requirement and a risk profile. Same questions, every time. **Spaces get a table.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Why the table is genuinely better here, not just tidier

Three reasons, all of them about the reader. **[PRINCIPLE]**

1. **Comparison becomes free.** A gaffer who wants to know which of nine spaces need a tungsten package reads one column. In prose, that is nine paragraph reads and a note pad.
2. **Gaps become visible.** An empty cell is an alarm. A paragraph that failed to mention the palette looks exactly like a paragraph that had nothing to say about it.
3. **Sorting and filtering become possible.** The 1st AD wants the spaces sorted by shoot day. The producer wants them sorted by risk. Prose does not sort.

### The failure mode in the other direction

Forcing genuinely dissimilar content into a table produces columns that are empty for most rows and a "notes" column that carries all the real information. **When one column holds most of the meaning, the table was the wrong form.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A6. ONE TABLE PER ORGANIZING AXIS

This is the most commonly missed idea in bible construction, and the most valuable.

### The claim

A production has more than one legitimate way to organize its facts. The two universal ones are:

- **The person axis:** the cast, the characters, the on-camera talent, the featured extras.
- **The space axis:** the locations, the sets, the zones within them.

These two tables share underlying facts. A person appears in a space. A space contains people. It is therefore extremely tempting to build one table and consider the other redundant. **[PRINCIPLE]**

**That is wrong, and it is wrong for an operational reason, not an aesthetic one: different departments schedule different things.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Why they are not duplicates

| | Person-centric table | Space-centric table |
|---|---|---|
| **Row identity** | One row per person or character | One row per location, set or named zone |
| **Primary consumers** | Casting, wardrobe, makeup and hair, agents and bookings, 1st AD for cast scheduling | Art, set dec, locations, lighting, grip, 1st AD for unit moves, safety |
| **The question it answers** | "What do we owe this person, and where do they go?" | "What do we build here, and what happens in it?" |
| **What it is sorted by in practice** | Screen time, cost, number of shoot days, availability | Shoot day, build lead time, risk, cost |
| **What is booked from it** | People, fittings, prosthetics, chaperones, doubles | Spaces, permits, trucks, build crews, gear packages |
| **What breaks if it is missing** | Someone arrives unprepped, or is booked for a day they are not needed | A space is under-dressed, wrongly lit, or arrives without a permit |

The two tables answer questions that are not transformations of each other. **You cannot derive the wardrobe count for a person by reading the space table, and you cannot derive the build lead time for a space by reading the person table.** They overlap in a few cells and diverge in most. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The scheduling argument, stated plainly

A 1st AD builds a schedule against two independent constraint sets: **people have availability, and spaces have access windows.** These constraints do not reduce to each other. A person is available Tuesday. A space is available Wednesday. Both facts are required, and neither can be inferred from the other. Any document that carries only one axis forces the AD to reconstruct the other by hand, which is exactly the work the bible exists to eliminate. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### When a third axis earns its own table

A third table is justified when **a distinct set of entities is scheduled, budgeted or assigned to an owner independently of both people and spaces.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

Common cases where a third axis earns a table:

| Axis | Rows | Why it does not fold into person or space |
|---|---|---|
| **Time / story day / sequence** | Story days, chapters, episodes, or day-parts | Continuity, wardrobe changes and light state are governed by story day, not by location. A single space shot across three story days has three different treatments |
| **Hero props, product or vehicles** | Each prop, product variant or vehicle | These are sourced, insured, transported and version-controlled on their own timeline. A product with six SKUs has six procurement facts that belong to neither a person nor a room |
| **Effects and stunts** | Each effect, gag or stunt beat | These carry their own crew, their own safety documentation, their own rehearsal requirement, and often their own separate permit |
| **Deliverables / cutdowns** | Each final asset, aspect ratio or language version | These are scheduled and approved in post, long after people and spaces are released |
| **Creatures, vehicles or entities in fantastical work** | Each non-human entity | They combine person-like and prop-like facts and fit cleanly in neither table |

The test to apply before adding a third table: **does this set of entities have at least three attributes that appear in no other table, and does at least one department schedule against it directly?** If no to either, it is a column somewhere, not a table. **[PRINCIPLE]**

The test to apply against adding a table: **every additional table multiplies sync cost.** Two tables have one sync relationship. Three have three. Four have six. Do not add an axis for elegance. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A7. THE SELF-CONTAINED CELL RULE

### The rule

**In a reference table that is looked up out of order, every cell must be readable on its own.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

No cell may say "same as row 3." No cell may say "see above." No cell may say "as previously described." No cell may say "standard treatment" unless "standard treatment" is defined in a named place that the cell names explicitly, and even then the cell should carry the substance.

### Why this feels wrong to good writers

Every instinct of good technical writing says the opposite. Do not repeat yourself. Define once, reference thereafter. Duplication is debt. Those instincts are correct in the domains where they were formed: software, where duplicated logic can silently diverge and the compiler will not warn you, and long-form prose, where a linear reader has already read the earlier passage and repetition insults them.

**Both of those preconditions fail in a reference table.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

- The linear-reader precondition fails because there is no linear reader. Row 3 may never be read. "Same as row 3" addresses a reader who does not exist.
- The maintenance argument is weaker than it looks, because the cost it is trading against is not aesthetic. It is a department buying the wrong thing.

### The information-loss argument

This is the part that matters most, and it is not obvious.

"Same as row 3" is not merely inconvenient. **It is usually information loss disguised as brevity.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

When a writer writes "same as the previous space," they almost never mean literally identical. They mean *similar in the respect I was thinking about at the time.* The two spaces may share a palette but not a floor treatment. They may share a lighting recipe but not a ceiling height. The shortcut collapses a real difference into a false equivalence, and it does so invisibly, because the reader has no way to know which respects were meant.

The clearest version of this failure: a cell that reads **"generic dressing."** To the writer that meant "nothing special, nothing hero, I have not thought about it yet." To the art director standing in a prop house with a budget, it means **nothing at all.** They cannot buy "generic." They will buy something, and it will be wrong, and the wrongness will be discovered on the monitor. **[PRINCIPLE]**

A self-contained cell is forced to say what is actually there: "four mismatched wooden chairs, one upholstered armchair with visible wear on the arms, a low table with rings on the surface, no soft furnishings." That is buyable. The act of writing the cell out in full is what surfaced the fact that nobody had decided. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The distinction that resolves the tension with DRY

There are two different things that look like repetition, and only one of them is a problem.

| | What it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Repeating a shape** | Every row has the same seven columns, and similar rows have similar-looking content | **Correct and required.** This is the template working. Shape repetition is what makes the table scannable and comparable |
| **Pointing rows at each other** | A cell whose content is a reference to another cell | **Incorrect.** This is a shortcut for the writer paid for by every reader, and it hides real differences |

Stated as a single sentence: **repeat the content, never the pointer.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Where the DRY instinct is still right

Self-containment applies to **cells in a reference table looked up out of order.** It does not license copying the same paragraph into six places in the body of the document. The single-source rule still governs narrative and policy content: the rules of the world, the safety policy, the approval chain. Those have one home and are linked, not duplicated. **[PRINCIPLE]**

The working boundary: **facts that are looked up per entity are duplicated into the entity's row. Facts that govern the whole production are stated once and referenced.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The cost of self-containment, and how to pay it

Self-containment increases the surface that has to be kept in sync. That cost is real and it is paid by the sync protocol in B7 and the consolidation pass in A11. **[PRINCIPLE]** It is the right trade because the failure mode it prevents is a department acting on a guess, and the failure mode it introduces is a document that disagrees with itself, which is at least detectable by audit.

## A8. THE MISSING-COLUMN PROBLEM

### The failure

A table is almost always built to answer one class of question, because it was built by one person with one problem in mind that week. A table built for scheduling will have shoot day, unit, cast required and access window. A table built for lighting will have time of day, practical sources, color temperature and rig notes.

**Both of those tables can be complete, correct, well maintained, and still not answer the question "what does this place physically look like."** **[PRINCIPLE]**

That is the missing-column problem: **a table quietly omits an entire class of question because no column was ever created to hold it.** And unlike an empty cell, a missing column is invisible. There is no blank to notice. The table looks finished. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Why it is hard to catch

Empty cells announce themselves. Missing columns do not, because the table's own structure defines what counts as complete. A table with no physical-description column will pass its own completeness check with a perfect score. **[PRINCIPLE]**

The only reliable detection method is external: **take the list of departments who will read this table and ask, for each one, what they need to extract. Any need that has no column is a missing column.** **[PRINCIPLE]** This is why the reader inventory in A1 is not decoration. It is the audit key.

### The folding error

The related and more insidious version: a distinct question is **folded into a neighbouring column** because the two seemed related.

Examples of folding that look reasonable and are not: **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Folded into | What got lost | Who suffers |
|---|---|---|
| Palette absorbs physical description | Materials, condition, scale, layout, what is actually in the room | Set dec, buyers, construction |
| Lighting recipe absorbs time of day and story day | Which story day this state belongs to, so continuity cannot be checked | Script supervisor, editor |
| Screen time absorbs shot count | Nine short shots and one long shot are the same total and completely different days | 1st AD, DP |
| Risk flag absorbs prep note | What to do about the risk, as opposed to that a risk exists | Whoever owns the fix |
| Wardrobe absorbs number of changes | The count that drives fittings, duplicates and laundry | Costume supervisor |
| Location absorbs zone | Which part of the location, which drives dressing extent and unit moves | Art, locations, AD |

**The rule: every distinct question gets its own column, even when the answer will sometimes be short and even when two columns will sometimes contain related content.** A column is cheap. A folded question is a department working blind. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The counter-pressure

Columns are not free forever. A table with twenty-eight columns is unreadable on any screen a crew member will have. The resolution is not to fold, it is to **split by axis** (A6) or to **split the table into a scannable master and a detail sheet per row.** The master answers "which," the detail answers "what exactly." **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A9. INTENTIONAL NON-ENTRIES

### The principle

**A blank cell and a cell that says "not applicable, because X" carry completely different information, and the difference is the difference between an open question and a closed one.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

A blank means one of at least four things, and the reader cannot tell which:

1. Nobody has decided yet.
2. Somebody decided it does not apply.
3. Somebody decided it does apply but forgot to write it.
4. It was deleted during an edit and nobody noticed.

Three of those four require action. One does not. **A blank forces the reader to find out which, and under time pressure they will guess.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The form of a good non-entry

A good non-entry has two parts: **the assertion that it does not apply, and the reason.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| (blank) | "No makeup treatment. Character is never seen closer than a wide two-shot" |
| "N/A" | "No wardrobe change. Single continuous story day" |
| "None" | "No practical sources. Space is lit entirely from outside the set through the window wall" |
| "TBC" | "Deferred. Awaiting client product decision, owner: producer, due: end of prep week 2" |

The reason is what makes the entry survive a change. If the framing rule in row one changes and someone widens that shot, the entry tells the reader immediately that the makeup decision was contingent on the framing and must now be revisited. A bare "N/A" tells them nothing and the decision silently becomes wrong. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The four flavors worth distinguishing

| Flavor | Meaning | What the reader should do |
|---|---|---|
| **Not applicable** | Structurally does not exist for this row, and here is why | Nothing. Closed |
| **Deliberately none** | It could exist, and it was decided against | Nothing, but do not add it back without asking |
| **Deferred** | It will exist, decision pending, with owner and date | Chase the owner if the date passes |
| **Unknown** | Nobody has looked at this yet | Escalate. This is a live gap |

Collapsing all four into "N/A" or into a blank destroys the distinction between a closed decision and an open risk, which is the single most useful thing a bible can tell a producer. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A10. PARALLEL TABLE DRIFT

### The failure

Once a bible has more than one master table, the same entity appears in more than one place. A person appears in the person table and is named in the space table. A space appears in the space table and is named in the person table and again in the art department entry.

**When a fact about that entity changes and only one appearance is updated, the document now contains a live contradiction.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

This is not a hypothetical. It is the single most common way a mature bible goes wrong, because it happens through normal, competent work: someone is asked a question, they answer it correctly, they update the table they were looking at, and they do not know or do not remember that the fact appears in three other places.

### Why it is worse than duplicated prose

Duplicated prose that drifts is usually noticeable, because prose carries context and a contradiction reads oddly. **A contradicting table cell reads completely normally**, because a cell has no context. Two cells saying "3 changes" and "4 changes" both look correct in isolation. Nothing about either one signals a problem. **[PRINCIPLE]**

And the consequence is direct: two departments act on two different facts, and both believe they are following the document.

### The three drift patterns

| Pattern | How it happens | Typical symptom |
|---|---|---|
| **Value drift** | The same attribute holds different values in two tables | Two departments prepare different quantities |
| **Existence drift** | An entity is removed from one table and retained in another | A person is cast out of the film and still appears in a space's cast list |
| **Naming drift** | The same entity is called two different things in two tables | Nobody can tell whether these are one entity or two, and the count is wrong |

Naming drift is the most dangerous, because it defeats every automated and manual reconciliation. **A single stable identifier per entity, used in every table, is the cheapest structural defence available.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The honest framing

**Parallel reference tables drift exactly the way duplicated prose drifts.** Building two tables is a decision to take on a sync obligation, and it is worth taking on because the two tables answer different questions (A6). But it must be taken on **knowingly, with a protocol** (B7), not as a side effect of someone making a second table because the first one got crowded. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A11. THE CONSOLIDATION PASS

### The condition it treats

Documents that live through several rounds of revision accumulate a characteristic geological structure. **[PRINCIPLE]**

1. **The original entry.** Written in round one, in the document's intended shape.
2. **The inline patch.** Round two, someone appends a correction to the end of the original entry rather than rewriting it, because rewriting felt riskier.
3. **The separate audit section.** Round three, someone runs a review and writes the findings in a new section at the end, because that is where the review happened.
4. **The cross-reference.** Round four, someone notices the original entry and the audit section disagree, and rather than resolving them, adds a pointer from one to the other.

At the end of this, **the same fact exists in four places, in four shapes, with four different degrees of currency, and the reader must read all four and perform their own reconciliation to find out what is true.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

Every individual step in that sequence was a reasonable, conservative decision by a competent person. The result is a document that has stopped being usable.

### The diagnostic

**When answering one question requires reading more than one location, the document needs consolidation rather than another patch.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

That is the whole test. It does not matter how well written the patches are.

### What the pass does

The consolidation pass is not a rewrite and it is not an edit for style. It is a **normalization**. **[PRINCIPLE]**

1. **Collect every location where the fact currently appears.** Original entry, patch note, audit section, cross-reference, and any department entry that restated it.
2. **Determine the current truth.** By recency of approval, not by which version is best written. Where two approved versions conflict, escalate rather than choose.
3. **Write it once, in the document's canonical shape**, in the place the architecture says it belongs.
4. **Delete the other locations entirely.** Not strike through, not "superseded, see above." Delete. A superseded entry left in place is a trap for the out-of-order reader, who has no reason to know it is superseded. **[PRINCIPLE]**
5. **Record what changed in the change log**, which is the one place where history is allowed to live.
6. **Re-run the sync protocol**, because consolidation is itself a change to the facts.

### The principle in one line

**Same information, one place, one consistent shape.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Why teams resist it

Consolidation destroys the visible record of who found what and when, which people are attached to, and it requires making a judgment about which version is true, which people avoid. Both concerns are legitimate and both are handled by the change log. **History belongs in the change log. The body of the document holds only the present.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

### When not to consolidate

Do not consolidate mid-shoot on a live document that crew are actively reading, unless the contradiction is causing harm. A restructure during production breaks everyone's mental map of where things are. **Consolidate at natural boundaries: end of prep, between blocks, between episodes, before a client review.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A12. HOW COMMERCIAL PRODUCTION BIBLES DIFFER FROM FEATURE AND SERIES BIBLES

All three do the same job. They differ in lifespan, in who has approval rights, and in what the bible is protecting. **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Dimension | Commercial / branded content | Feature film | Television / streaming series |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lifespan** | Weeks. Often a single shoot and its cutdowns | Months to years, but finite and closed | Years, across seasons, with rotating personnel |
| **Length** | 10 to 40 pages typically | Can run to hundreds of pages | Grows continuously, often becomes a wiki |
| **Primary risk it manages** | Brand compliance and a fixed runtime | Consistency across a large crew and a long build | Consistency across time and staff turnover |
| **Who holds final approval** | The client, through the agency. Approval is external and can arrive late | The director and studio. Approval is internal | The showrunner. Approval is internal and continuous |
| **Non-negotiable content** | Brand rules: logo, color values, product handling, legal and regulatory claims, mandatory disclaimers | Story and design coherence | Series canon and character rules |
| **Governing constraint** | Duration to the frame, and the deliverable matrix of cuts, ratios and languages | Story, schedule and budget | Format, episode count and returning-series continuity |
| **Table axes usually required** | People, spaces, **product or hero item**, **deliverables** | People, spaces, story days, effects | People, spaces, story days, recurring sets, canon rules |
| **Who reads it after wrap** | Almost nobody, except for the next campaign in the same world | Marketing, archive, franchise planning | Every incoming writer and director, indefinitely |
| **Update cadence** | Daily during prep, then frozen | Weekly, then locked per block | Continuous, versioned per season |
| **Failure signature** | A shot that violates a brand rule and cannot be used, discovered in post | A design inconsistency visible on screen | A canon contradiction that fans and staff both notice |

### The three practical consequences for commercial work

1. **The product or hero item almost always earns its own table.** It has variants, procurement lead times, handling rules, legal constraints and approval chains that belong to neither a person nor a space. **[PRINCIPLE]**
2. **The deliverables matrix is part of the bible, not an afterthought.** A single shoot may owe a dozen cuts across ratios and languages, each with different framing requirements that must be known before the camera rolls. Safe-area and framing rules for vertical cutdowns are decisions made in prep or they are not made at all. **[PRINCIPLE]**
3. **The bible carries the client's non-negotiables verbatim.** Paraphrasing a brand rule is how a campaign gets rejected. Brand mandatories are quoted, attributed and dated, not summarized. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### The one thing commercial bibles get right that longer forms often do not

Because they are short-lived and read under extreme time pressure, commercial bibles are usually forced into good information architecture: tables, one page per department, no narrative. The discipline is imposed by the format. Longer-form bibles have room to sprawl and frequently use it. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## A13. VOCABULARY GLOSSARY

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Production bible / crew bible** | The consolidated reference document holding every cross-department decision for a production |
| **World bible** | The subset covering the invented or established world: geography, history, rules, culture, technology |
| **Series bible / show bible** | The episodic equivalent, doubling as a pitch document and a continuity reference for incoming writers and directors |
| **Style guide** | The visual-rules subset, used where third parties must reproduce a look without contact with the original team |
| **Master reference table** | A table with one row per entity of a given type and one column per question asked of every entity of that type |
| **Organizing axis** | The entity type a table is built around: people, spaces, story days, props, deliverables |
| **Row identity** | The stable identifier for a row, used unchanged across every table |
| **Self-contained cell** | A cell that can be read and acted on without reading any other cell |
| **Pointer cell** | A cell whose content is a reference to another cell. Prohibited in reference tables |
| **Intentional non-entry** | An explicit statement that an attribute does not apply, with the reason |
| **Missing column** | An entire class of question the table has no place to answer. Invisible to the table's own completeness check |
| **Folding** | Absorbing a distinct question into a neighbouring column because the two seemed related |
| **Parallel table drift** | Divergence between two tables that hold the same fact about the same entity |
| **Value / existence / naming drift** | The three forms drift takes: wrong value, entity present in one table only, entity named differently in two tables |
| **Consolidation pass** | Rewriting accumulated patches, audits and cross-references into one authoritative statement in the canonical shape |
| **Change log** | The single location where superseded history is allowed to live |
| **Zone** | A named sub-area of a location, treated as its own row when it is dressed, lit or scheduled separately |
| **Story day** | The in-fiction day a scene occurs on. Governs wardrobe, continuity and light state independently of shoot day |
| **Shoot day** | The real calendar day of production. Governs booking and logistics |
| **Deliverable matrix** | The full set of final assets owed, across cuts, durations, aspect ratios and languages |
| **Brand mandatory** | A client rule that cannot be varied. Quoted verbatim, attributed and dated |
| **Out-of-order reader** | A specialist who opens the document at one section, at an unpredictable time, and reads nothing else |
| **Comparing reader** | A reader consuming two or more sections at once. The reason templates must be consistent |
| **Canonical shape** | The agreed structure a given kind of entry takes throughout the document |
| **Single sourcing** | The rule that a governing fact has exactly one authoritative home |

## B1. THE QUESTIONS THIS ROLE ASKS BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING

The agent does not begin drafting until these are answered or explicitly deferred with an owner. A bible built on assumptions becomes a bible that departments stop trusting, and a distrusted bible is worse than none because it still absorbs time. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### On purpose and readership

1. Who are the named readers, department by department? List them. This list is the audit key for the missing-column check (B8).
2. Which of them will read it on a phone, on a set, under time pressure? That governs table width.
3. Is this bible a pitch artifact, a production artifact, or both? A document that must persuade and must also be looked up needs the persuasion in a front section and the reference behind it, never interleaved.
4. Who owns it? One name. Who approves changes to it? One name. These may differ.
5. What is the version and freeze policy? When does it lock, and what happens to changes after lock?

### On scope

6. What is in scope and what is explicitly not? Written down, because scope creep in a bible is silent.
7. Is there an existing brand guideline, franchise canon or prior bible this must not contradict? Where does it live and who arbitrates conflicts?
8. Which decisions are already locked and which are live? Locked decisions get stated. Live ones get an owner and a date.

### On the axes

9. What entity types exist here? Enumerate them, do not assume.
10. Which of them have at least three attributes unique to them and at least one department scheduling directly against them? Those become tables. Everything else becomes a column.
11. Is there a hero product, vehicle, creature or effect set? If yes, it almost certainly needs its own table.
12. Does the story span more than one story day, or the shoot more than one unit? If yes, a time axis may be required.
13. What is the stable identifier scheme for each entity type? Decide this before the first row is written. Renaming later is where naming drift is born.

### On the entities themselves

14. How many people, named and unnamed? Are background and featured background distinguished?
15. How many spaces, and are they subdivided into zones? A location with three distinctly dressed areas is three rows, not one.
16. Which spaces are build, which are found, which are existing standing sets? Different lead times, different columns.
17. What is the total runtime or page count, and how is it allocated across spaces and people? Screen time is a column in both master tables and it must sum.

### On constraints

18. What are the non-negotiables? Brand mandatories, legal or regulatory requirements, safety rules, canon.
19. What is the deliverable matrix? Every cut, duration, ratio and language. This determines framing rules that must be known in prep.
20. What is the approval chain and its latency? A client who takes five days to approve changes the definition of a deferred entry.

### On maintenance

21. Who runs the sync protocol after a change, and how often?
22. Where does the change log live?
23. What is the consolidation cadence, and what are the natural boundaries at which restructuring is permitted?

## B2. THE PER-DEPARTMENT ENTRY TEMPLATE

Every department entry uses these fields, in this order, with these names, every time, with no exceptions. Fields that do not apply carry an intentional non-entry (A9), never a blank. **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Field | What it holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Department** | The department name as used everywhere else in the document | Must match the name used in every table and the contact sheet exactly |
| **Head of department** | One name, one contact, one deputy | If unfilled, state "vacant, owner of the fill: X, due: date". Never blank |
| **Scope: owns** | What this department is responsible for delivering | Written as deliverables, not activities. "Six dressed zones" not "set dressing" |
| **Scope: does not own** | What a reader might reasonably assume this department owns and it does not | This field prevents the most common on-set argument. It is not optional |
| **Governing rules** | The non-negotiables that constrain this department | Brand mandatories quoted verbatim with attribution and date. Safety rules stated in full |
| **Entities involved** | Which rows of which master tables this department touches | By stable identifier, so the sync protocol can find them |
| **Key decisions made** | The settled creative and technical decisions | Each one stated as an instruction, not as taste. Convert "warm" into a value |
| **Open decisions** | What is still undecided | Each with an owner and a due date. Anything without an owner is escalated, not recorded |
| **Dependencies: needs from others** | What this department cannot start without, and from whom | Named department, named artifact, named date |
| **Dependencies: owes others** | What other departments are waiting on from this one | Same format |
| **Lead times** | The longest lead item and its ordering deadline | This is what makes a schedule real. One line minimum |
| **Budget owner and envelope** | Who holds the money and what the number is | If confidential, state "held by X, not published here", never blank |
| **Risk flags** | Things that can go wrong, with likelihood and consequence | Each flag names the department that would absorb the consequence |
| **Prep milestones** | What must be true by when | Dates, not phases |
| **On-day protocol** | What this department does on a shoot day, and who they report to | Written for someone who has never worked with this crew |
| **Anything else** | The open field | Exists so that real content is never smuggled into the wrong field to satisfy the form |

**Enforcement rules for the agent:**

1. Field names never vary between departments. Not "HOD" in one and "Department head" in another.
2. Field order never varies.
3. A department entry with a blank field is incomplete, not finished. Blanks are findings.
4. If a department genuinely has no risk flags, the entry says "No risk flags identified as of [date], reviewed by [name]." That is an intentional non-entry with an owner, and it is auditable. A blank is not.

## B3. THE CAST AND CHARACTER MASTER TABLE

One row per person or character who appears on camera. Featured background gets rows. Crowd background gets a single summary row per crowd group with a count. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Column definitions

| Column | Definition | Why it exists | Self-containment rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ID** | Stable identifier, never reused, never changed | The join key for every other table and every department entry | Must be identical in every table |
| **Name / character** | Both the fictional name and the performer name once cast | Casting, wardrobe, makeup and the AD all address the person by one of these two | Carry both. "TBC" in the performer field is a deferred non-entry with an owner |
| **Role and function** | What this person is in the story and what they do | Casting brief, direction, and the justification for their screen time | Written in full. Never "as above" |
| **Screen time** | Total intended seconds or minutes on camera | Governs how much prep is justified, and it must sum to the runtime across the table | A number, not "brief" or "significant" |
| **Shot references** | The shot numbers or timecode ranges this person appears in | The lookup that tells every department when this person is needed | Full list. Never a range that hides gaps |
| **Spaces appeared in** | The IDs of every space this person appears in | The cross-axis link. Drives unit scheduling and the sync protocol | By ID plus name, so the cell is readable without opening the other table |
| **Wardrobe: description** | What this person actually wears, per change, described concretely | Costume sourcing, fittings, budget | Materials, colors, condition, fit. Never "casual" or "standard" |
| **Wardrobe: number of changes** | The count of distinct looks | Drives fittings, duplicates, laundry and continuity. Folding this into the description loses it | An integer |
| **Wardrobe: continuity notes** | What must match between which shots, and any damage, wetting or aging progression | Script supervisor and costume standby | States the shots by number |
| **Makeup and hair treatment** | The specific treatment: base, aging, prosthetics, condition, hair state | Prep time on the day, prosthetic lead times, artist count | Never "natural". State what natural means here |
| **Makeup and hair: prep time** | Minutes required in the chair, per look | The number that sets the call time | An integer per look |
| **Location treatment for this person** | How this person is handled in each space they appear in: where they stand, what the space does to them, any per-space variation | This is the cell most often collapsed into the space table and lost. A person can be treated differently in two spaces | One line per space, each naming the space |
| **Physical and demographic requirements** | Age range, build, skills, languages, physical capability required | Casting brief and safety | Concrete. "Must be able to run 40 metres repeatedly" not "athletic" |
| **Availability and booking** | Days booked, days available, agent or contact, chaperone requirements for minors | AD scheduling and legal compliance | Dates, not "flexible" |
| **Cost** | Fee, buyout, usage, overtime terms | Producer. Also the answer to "can we add a day" | Currency and terms, or "held by producer, not published here" |
| **Doubles / stand-ins / stunts** | Whether this person requires a double, a stand-in, or a stunt performer, and who | Separate bookings, separate wardrobe duplicates, separate insurance | Names or "none required, because [reason]" |
| **Risk flag** | Anything that can go wrong with this person | Producer and AD | Names the consequence and the department that absorbs it |
| **Prep note** | What must happen before shoot day | The action list | Owner and date |
| **Status** | Locked / provisional / deferred / cut | Tells the reader how much to trust the row | One of four values, never blank |

### Rules of use

1. **Every cell is self-contained.** No "same as row 4". If two performers wear the same jacket, both rows describe the jacket.
2. **Screen time across the table is reconciled against the runtime and the reconciliation is stated below the table.** An unstated sum is an unchecked sum.
3. **A cut character is not deleted.** The row remains with status "cut" and a date. Deleting it causes existence drift with every other table that still names them.
4. **A crowd summary row carries a count, a description and a wardrobe brief.** "Extras" is not a row.

## B4. THE LOCATIONS AND SETS MASTER TABLE

One row per space that is separately dressed, separately lit, or separately scheduled. A location with three such areas produces three rows, each named as a zone of that location. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Column definitions

| Column | Definition | Why it exists | Self-containment rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ID** | Stable identifier, never reused | Join key for the cast table, the shot list and every department entry | Identical everywhere |
| **Zone name** | The name this space is called on every document, including the call sheet | Everyone must use one name for one place. Naming drift starts here | One canonical name. Aliases listed once, in this cell |
| **Parent location** | The physical location this zone belongs to | Drives unit moves, permits, transport and catering | Full address or "studio, stage X" |
| **Physical description** | What this place actually looks like: architecture, dimensions, materials, floor, walls, ceiling, windows, condition, scale, what is in it | **This is the column most often missing entirely.** It is what set dec, buyers and construction act on | Concrete and buyable. Never "generic", never "standard", never "typical" |
| **Dressing detail** | The specific items to be sourced, built or removed | The shopping list | Itemized. A count and a description per item |
| **Shots / timecode** | The shot numbers or timecode ranges that occur here | The link to the shot list. Drives how much of the space is seen | Enumerated, not summarized |
| **Screen time** | Total seconds this space is on camera | Justifies build spend. A space with four seconds does not get a full build | A number |
| **Extent seen** | Which walls, which directions, what is in frame and what never is | The single largest cost lever in art department. A room seen on one wall is a wall, not a room | Named directions or wall references |
| **Lighting recipe** | The intended lighting approach: key direction, quality, contrast, color temperature values, practical sources, time of day feel | Gaffer and DP prep, rig orders, power | Values and sources. Never "moody" alone |
| **Practical sources** | The in-shot light sources that must exist physically | Art must supply them, electrics must control them. Separate from the recipe because two departments own them | Itemized with type and count |
| **Color accents** | The specific palette for this space: dominant, secondary, accent, with values where the brand or grade requires it | Art, wardrobe conflict checking, color grade | Named colors or values. Never "warm tones" alone |
| **People present** | The IDs and names of everyone appearing in this space | Cross-axis link. Drives the AD's cast-per-unit calculation | ID plus name, readable without the cast table |
| **Story day / time of day** | The in-fiction day and time | Continuity, wardrobe state, light state | Explicit. A space used across two story days gets two rows or a clearly split cell |
| **Shoot day / unit** | The real day and unit | Scheduling and logistics | Date or "unscheduled, owner: AD" |
| **Access and permissions** | Permit status, access windows, restrictions, noise limits, who the contact is | The constraint that most often breaks a schedule late | Status plus contact plus date |
| **Prep note** | What must happen before the crew arrives: build, dress, strip, pre-light, recce | The action list | Owner and date per item |
| **Strike and restore** | What must be returned to its original state and to what standard | The obligation that gets forgotten and costs money | Explicit, or "none required, because [reason]" |
| **Risk flag** | Safety, weather, access, structural, crowd, legal, or cost risk | Producer, AD, safety officer | Names the risk, the likelihood, the consequence and the department that absorbs it |
| **Cost and lead time** | Build or hire cost and the longest lead item | Producer and construction | Number plus the ordering deadline |
| **Status** | Locked / provisional / scouting / deferred / dropped | How much to trust the row | One of five values, never blank |

### Rules of use

1. **A dropped space keeps its row with status "dropped" and a date.** Deleting it causes existence drift.
2. **The physical description column is mandatory and is audited separately**, because it is the column most likely to be missing or folded (A8).
3. **Screen time across the space table and screen time across the cast table are two different sums** and both are reconciled against the runtime. They will not match each other and should not be expected to, because more than one person can be in a space at once.
4. **Zone splitting is decided once and recorded.** If a location becomes three zones halfway through prep, that is a change that triggers the sync protocol against every table and every department entry.

## B5. THE THIRD-AXIS DECISION PROCEDURE

Before adding any table beyond people and spaces, the agent runs this test and records the result. **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Step | Question | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is there a set of entities here that is neither a person nor a space? | Enumerable, with at least three members |
| 2 | Does it have at least three attributes that appear in no existing table? | Listed explicitly |
| 3 | Does at least one department schedule, budget or book against it directly? | Named department, named activity |
| 4 | Would folding it into an existing table produce a column that is empty for most rows? | Yes means it needs its own table |
| 5 | Is the sync cost acceptable? | Count the new sync relationships: adding the Nth table adds N minus 1 relationships |

If steps 1 through 4 pass and step 5 is accepted, build the table. Otherwise it is a column, or a detail sheet hanging off an existing row.

**Standard third axes and their trigger conditions:**

| Axis | Build it when |
|---|---|
| Story day | The story spans more than one day, or wardrobe or light state changes within a location |
| Hero product / prop / vehicle | Any item has variants, a procurement lead time, handling rules or its own approval chain |
| Effects and stunts | Any beat requires separate safety documentation, rehearsal or specialist crew |
| Deliverables | More than one final cut, ratio, duration or language is owed |
| Non-human entities | Creatures, robots or animals that carry both person-like and prop-like facts |

## B6. THE CONSOLIDATION TRIGGER LIST

The agent proposes a consolidation pass (A11) when any of these signals is present. Two or more signals makes it mandatory before the next external distribution. **[PRINCIPLE]**

| # | Signal | Why it indicates consolidation rather than another patch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Answering one question requires reading two or more locations | The defining diagnostic. Nothing else needs to be true |
| 2 | The document contains a section whose title includes "update", "revision", "addendum", "correction" or "note on" | These are patch artifacts by definition |
| 3 | A section exists whose content is entirely findings about another section | The audit section pattern. Findings belong merged into the entries they concern |
| 4 | Any cell or paragraph points to another cell or paragraph instead of stating a fact | Pointer content is a symptom of a writer avoiding a rewrite |
| 5 | The same entity is described in three or more places | Beyond two, drift is a near certainty |
| 6 | Two locations state different values for the same attribute | A live contradiction. Consolidation is the fix, not another cross-reference |
| 7 | Strike-through, "superseded", "was X now Y" or "ignore the above" appears anywhere | History has leaked into the body. It belongs in the change log |
| 8 | The table of contents no longer predicts where a reader will find something | The architecture has drifted from the map |
| 9 | New content is being appended to the end because the writer is unsure where it belongs | The document has stopped having a shape |
| 10 | A reader has asked "which version of this is right" more than once | The document has lost authority, which is its only real asset |
| 11 | Field names or column names vary between comparable sections | Shape consistency has broken. The comparing reader is now paying a tax |
| 12 | The change log has entries that were never reflected in the body | Changes are being recorded instead of applied |

### The consolidation output

Every consolidation pass produces, in this order:

1. **The list of facts consolidated**, by entity ID and attribute.
2. **The locations collapsed**, explicitly named, so a reader looking for an old section knows it was deliberately removed.
3. **The conflicts found and how each was resolved**, with the basis for resolution.
4. **The conflicts found and escalated**, with the owner and the decision required.
5. **The change log entries written.**
6. **The sync protocol result** run after consolidation.

## B7. THE CROSS-TABLE SYNC PROTOCOL

Run after **any** change to any fact about any entity. Not weekly, not at milestones. After every change. **[PRINCIPLE]**

The protocol exists because the natural failure is not carelessness. It is a competent person correctly updating the one table they happened to be looking at.

### Procedure

| Step | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identify the entity by ID**, not by name. Names drift, IDs do not | The ID |
| 2 | **Search every table for that ID** and for every alias of that name recorded in the document | The full list of appearances |
| 3 | **Search every department entry** for the ID and the name | The full list of narrative appearances |
| 4 | **Search the shot list, schedule, call sheets and any external artifact** derived from the bible | The downstream list |
| 5 | **For each appearance, ask whether the changed fact is present there.** Presence is not always literal. A change to a person's number of wardrobe changes affects the cast table cell, the wardrobe department entry, the fittings schedule and the budget | The affected set |
| 6 | **Update every affected appearance in the same working session.** Never defer one | Updated document |
| 7 | **Check for consequential facts.** A change to screen time changes the sum. A change to a space's extent seen changes the dressing budget. A change to a story day changes continuity | The consequence list |
| 8 | **Re-run the sums.** Screen time totals, shot counts, change counts, cast counts per space | Reconciliation statement |
| 9 | **Write one change log entry** naming the entity, the attribute, the old value, the new value, the date, the requester and every location updated | Change log entry |
| 10 | **Notify every department named in the affected set** in a single message that states the change and its consequence for them specifically | Notification |

### The cross-axis rule

**A change on one axis almost always has a consequence on the other.** **[PRINCIPLE]** The agent checks the paired axis by default:

| Change on the person axis | Mandatory check on the space axis |
|---|---|
| A person is added, cut or recast | Every space's "people present" cell |
| A person's shots change | Every space's shot list and screen time |
| A person's wardrobe changes | Any space whose palette could now clash |
| A person's availability changes | Every space scheduled on those days |

| Change on the space axis | Mandatory check on the person axis |
|---|---|
| A space is added, dropped or re-zoned | Every person's "spaces appeared in" and "location treatment" cells |
| A space's shoot day changes | Every person's booking |
| A space's palette changes | Every person's wardrobe in that space |
| A space's lighting recipe changes | Every person's makeup treatment where the light drives it |

### The naming defence

If any appearance is found under a different name for the same entity, the sync protocol does not just update it. **It normalizes the name to the canonical one everywhere and records the alias in the entity's row.** Leaving an alias in place guarantees the next sync will miss it. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## B8. THE SELF-CONTAINED-CELL AUDIT

Run before every distribution and after every consolidation.

### Detection: the shorthand patterns

The agent scans every cell in every table for these patterns. This list is the working detector. **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Pattern class | Example phrasings |
|---|---|
| **Explicit pointers** | "same as", "as above", "as below", "see row", "see section", "per the", "refer to", "ditto", "idem" |
| **Implicit pointers** | "likewise", "similarly", "also", "the same", "unchanged", "as before", "as previously", "matching" |
| **Placeholder vagueness** | "generic", "standard", "typical", "usual", "normal", "basic", "simple", "appropriate", "as needed", "as required", "suitable" |
| **Empty qualifiers** | "natural", "casual", "clean", "modern", "warm", "cool", "neutral" used alone with no value or specification |
| **Deferred without owner** | "TBC", "TBD", "pending", "to come", "?" with no name and no date |
| **Blanks** | Any empty cell |

### Expansion procedure

For each detection:

1. **Find what the pointer refers to.** If it cannot be determined, that is a finding in itself and it is escalated, not guessed.
2. **Ask in what respects the two entities are actually the same**, attribute by attribute. This is the step that surfaces the hidden difference. In practice, "same as row 3" is fully true less often than not.
3. **Write the full content into the cell.** Not a summary of it. The content.
4. **Record the differences discovered** as a finding, because a discovered difference means somebody would have acted on a false equivalence.
5. **For placeholder vagueness, do not expand by inventing.** "Generic dressing" means nobody decided. The correct output is a question to the owner, not a plausible-sounding invention. Inventing content to satisfy the audit is the worst possible outcome, because it looks decided and is not. **[PRINCIPLE]**
6. **For deferred entries, attach an owner and a date** and convert the cell to the deferred non-entry form (A9).
7. **For blanks, determine which of the four flavors applies** (A9) and write the corresponding intentional non-entry.

### Audit output format

```
SELF-CONTAINED-CELL AUDIT, [document], [version], [date]

POINTER CELLS FOUND: n
  [Table] [Row ID] [Column]: "[cell content]"
    Refers to: [target]
    Actually identical in: [attributes]
    Actually differs in: [attributes]  <-- FINDING
    Expanded to: [new content]

VAGUE CELLS FOUND: n
  [Table] [Row ID] [Column]: "[cell content]"
    Blocked department: [who cannot act on this]
    Question to owner: [the specific question]
    Owner: [name]  Due: [date]

BLANKS FOUND: n
  [Table] [Row ID] [Column]
    Resolved as: [not applicable / deliberately none / deferred / unknown]

DIFFERENCES DISCOVERED DURING EXPANSION: n
  [each one, because each was a latent wrong decision]
```

The "differences discovered" count is the number that justifies the audit. It is normally not zero. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## B9. THE MISSING-COLUMN AUDIT

A separate audit, because the self-contained-cell audit cannot detect it. **[PRINCIPLE]**

### Procedure

1. **Take the reader inventory** built in B1 question 1.
2. **For each reader, list the questions they will bring to this table.** Not what they might like to know. What they cannot act without.
3. **Map each question to a column.** Any question with no column is a missing column.
4. **For each mapped question, verify the column actually answers it rather than a neighbouring question.** This detects folding (A8).
5. **Add the columns.** If the table becomes too wide to scan, split into a scannable master plus a detail sheet per row. Do not fold.

### The standing question checklist

Every master table is checked against these six classes of question, because each one is a class that is routinely omitted. **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Class | The question | Commonly folded into |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | What is this thing called, everywhere, unambiguously | Nothing. Usually present |
| **Physical reality** | What does it actually look like, consist of, or wear | Palette, mood, or omitted entirely |
| **Quantity** | How many, how long, how much | Description |
| **Time** | When on the schedule, when in the story | Each other |
| **Ownership** | Who is responsible, who pays, who approves | Notes |
| **Consequence** | What breaks, what it risks, what must happen before | Notes |

If a table has no column that answers "what does this actually look like" and no column that answers "who is responsible", it is not finished regardless of how complete it appears.

## B10. FAILURE MODES AND UNDER-SPECIFICATION TELLS

| Failure mode | The tell in the document | What it costs | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Written for a linear reader** | Sections that begin "as we established above" | Every out-of-order reader is lost | Rewrite each section to open with its own subject |
| **Bespoke department entries** | Field names vary between departments | The comparing reader misses flags | Impose the B2 template |
| **Prose where a table belongs** | Three sections describing the same entity set from different angles | No comparison, no sorting, invisible gaps | Build the table, rows are entities, columns are the angles |
| **Table where prose belongs** | One "notes" column carrying most of the meaning | A table that must be read as prose anyway | Convert to templated sections |
| **Single-axis document** | Only a cast table, or only a locations table | The AD reconstructs the missing axis by hand | Build the second axis |
| **Pointer cells** | "same as", "as above", "ditto" | A department acts on a false equivalence | Expand every one (B8) |
| **Placeholder cells** | "generic", "standard", "typical" | The buyer buys the wrong thing | Escalate as a question, never invent |
| **Missing column** | Table looks complete and a department still asks a question | Silent, until it is expensive | Run B9 against the reader inventory |
| **Folded column** | Two questions sharing one cell | The weaker question loses | Split the column |
| **Blanks instead of non-entries** | Empty cells | Open questions look like closed ones | Resolve each into one of four flavors |
| **Parallel drift** | Two tables, same entity, different value | Two departments prepare differently, both correctly | Run the sync protocol, then fix the process |
| **Naming drift** | Same entity under two names | Counts are wrong and reconciliation is impossible | Canonical name, aliases recorded, IDs everywhere |
| **Existence drift** | Entity in one table, absent from another | People booked for spaces that no longer exist | Never delete rows, set status instead |
| **Patch geology** | Original entry, patch note, audit section, cross-reference | Readers reconcile the document themselves and get it wrong | Consolidation pass |
| **History in the body** | Strike-throughs and "superseded" | Out-of-order readers act on dead content | Move to change log, delete from body |
| **Unowned deferrals** | "TBC" with no name | Nothing ever resolves | Owner and date on every deferral |
| **Unstated sums** | Screen time column with no total | The allocation does not add up and nobody knows | State the reconciliation under every table |
| **Taste not converted** | "Warm", "premium", "authentic" as instructions | Every department interprets differently | Convert to values, materials and named references |
| **Brand rules paraphrased** | Mandatories in the bible's own voice | A rejected campaign | Quote verbatim, attribute, date |
| **Version ambiguity** | Multiple files, no version marker | Crew work from different documents | One version marker on every page, one distribution channel |
| **Silent restructure** | Sections renumbered mid-shoot | Everyone's mental map breaks at once | Restructure only at boundaries |

## B11. TRIGGER LIST: WHAT FORCES WHICH TABLES AND ENTRIES TO BE RE-OPENED

The left column is an upstream change. Every cell to the right must be re-opened, checked and either updated or explicitly confirmed unchanged. **Confirmed unchanged is a recorded result, not a skipped step.** **[PRINCIPLE]**

| Upstream change | Cast table | Locations table | Third-axis tables | Department entries | Sums to re-run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Script or storyboard revision** | All rows: shots, screen time, spaces | All rows: shots, screen time, extent seen | All | All | Screen time, shot count |
| **A character is added or cut** | The row plus every row sharing a space | Every "people present" cell | Effects, props if they carried the item | Casting, wardrobe, makeup, AD, budget | Cast count, screen time |
| **A performer is recast** | The row: name, physicals, availability, cost, prep times | Nothing structural, but check zone treatment | Doubles, stunts | Casting, wardrobe, makeup, transport | Cost |
| **A location is lost or changed** | Every row naming that space, including location treatment | The row plus any re-zoning | Effects, vehicles tied to the space | Art, locations, lighting, transport, AD, safety | Screen time by space, build cost |
| **A location is re-zoned** | "Spaces appeared in" on every affected row | Split into new rows with new IDs | Anything referencing the old ID | Art, AD, lighting | Space count, screen time |
| **Runtime or duration changes** | Screen time on every row | Screen time on every row | Deliverables | Editor, producer, client | Both screen time sums |
| **The deliverable matrix changes** | Framing implications per person | Extent seen, safe areas | Deliverables table | Camera, VFX, post, client | Deliverable count |
| **Palette or brand guideline update** | Wardrobe on every row | Color accents on every row | Product table | Art, wardrobe, color, VFX | Nothing numeric, everything visual |
| **Lighting approach changes** | Makeup where light drives it | Lighting recipe, practical sources | Effects | Lighting, grip, art, makeup | Power, rig cost |
| **Schedule or shoot day changes** | Availability and booking per person | Shoot day, access windows | All scheduled axes | AD, transport, catering, all HODs | Day count, cost |
| **Story day changes** | Wardrobe changes, continuity notes | Story day, time of day, light state | Story day table | Wardrobe, makeup, script supervisor, lighting | Change count |
| **Budget cut** | Cost, doubles, prep times | Cost, lead time, build versus found, extent seen | All | All | Every cost column |
| **A product or hero item changes** | Anyone handling it | Any space it appears in | The product table | Art, props, legal, client | Procurement lead times |
| **A safety or legal constraint arrives** | Physical requirements, stunts, minors | Access, permissions, risk flags | Effects and stunts | Safety, production, legal, AD | Nothing numeric |
| **A consolidation pass is run** | Every row touched | Every row touched | Every row touched | Every entry touched | All sums |
| **A naming convention changes** | Every row | Every row | Every row | Every entry, plus every downstream artifact | Nothing numeric, but re-run every count to verify |

### The re-derivation statement

Every version of the bible carries, immediately under the master tables, a short block that states the current arithmetic in plain sight. **[PRINCIPLE]**

```
RECONCILIATION, version [n], [date]
  Runtime target:            [value]
  Screen time, cast table:   [sum]  (people overlap, sum exceeds runtime by design)
  Screen time, space table:  [sum]  vs runtime: [delta]
  Cast rows:                 [n active] [n cut, retained]
  Space rows:                [n active] [n dropped, retained]
  Shots referenced, cast:    [n]  vs shot list: [n]  [match / MISMATCH]
  Shots referenced, spaces:  [n]  vs shot list: [n]  [match / MISMATCH]
  Open deferrals:            [n]  ([n] without owner  <-- escalate)
  Blanks:                    [n]  (target: 0)
  Pointer cells:             [n]  (target: 0)
```

A mismatch left in this block is acceptable. A missing block is not, because it means nobody checked. **[PRINCIPLE]**

## B14. STANDARD TENSIONS AND TYPICAL RESOLUTIONS

| Tension | Typical resolution |
|---|---|
| Self-containment produces a long, repetitive table | Accept it. Split into a scannable master and per-row detail sheets rather than reintroducing pointers |
| A department wants its section written in its own idiom | Keep the field names and order fixed, let the content inside a field take any form, and use the open field |
| Two tables disagree and both were recently edited | The most recently approved change wins, not the most recent edit. If both were approved, escalate. Never pick the better-written one |
| The bible is too long to read | Correct. It was never meant to be read. Fix navigation, not length |
| Someone wants to delete a cut character or dropped location | Set status, never delete. Deletion causes existence drift |
| A vague cell has no owner willing to decide | Record it as "unknown", name the department blocked by it, and escalate. Do not invent content |
| Consolidation is requested mid-shoot | Consolidate only the contradiction causing harm. Defer the structural pass to a boundary |
| A third axis is proposed for elegance | Run B5. If it fails any of steps 1 through 4, it is a column |
| The client changes a mandatory late | Quote the new mandatory verbatim with its date, run the full trigger list, and state which decisions it invalidates before agreeing to anything |
| A column is added and immediately empty for most rows | Check whether it belongs to a different axis. Empty-for-most is the signature of a wrong-table column |

## B15. THE AGENT'S STANDING BEHAVIOURAL RULES

1. **Assume nobody reads in order.** Every section, every row, every cell must work alone.
2. **Repeat the content, never the pointer.** Shape repetition is correct. Cross-references between rows are not.
3. **A blank is a finding.** Resolve every blank into not applicable, deliberately none, deferred, or unknown, and give the last two an owner.
4. **Never invent to fill a gap.** A vague cell means a decision was not made. Surface it as a question. Invented content that looks decided is the most damaging output this role can produce.
5. **Every distinct question gets its own column.** Columns are cheap. A folded question is a department working blind.
6. **Check the reader inventory against the column list before every distribution.** It is the only detector for missing columns.
7. **Two axes minimum.** People and spaces are scheduled independently by different departments and neither derives from the other.
8. **One ID per entity, used everywhere, never reused, never renamed silently.**
9. **Sync after every change, not on a cadence.** Check the paired axis by default.
10. **Confirmed unchanged is a result.** Record it. Skipping a check is not the same as running it.
11. **History lives in the change log. The body holds only the present.** Delete superseded content, do not strike it through.
12. **When answering one question needs two locations, stop patching and consolidate.**
13. **State the arithmetic in plain sight.** Every version carries a reconciliation block, mismatches included.
14. **Convert taste into instructions.** If a cell cannot be acted on by someone who has never met the director, it is not finished.
15. **Every finding names the department it blocks.** A gap without a consequence gets ignored. A gap with a named department gets fixed.
